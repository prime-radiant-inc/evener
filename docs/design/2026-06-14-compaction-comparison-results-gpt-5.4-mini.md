## Interpretation (hand-authored)

Re-run of the controlled eval with a **weaker summarizer (gpt-5.4-mini)** to test the
hypothesis that a weaker model would drop facts and thereby reveal the note's insurance
value. **It did not.** gpt-5.4-mini retained the single key fact in **all 22 cases**
(A retention 1.00, zero baseline-drops) — the same as gpt-5.5. The note's A→B gap is
still **+0.00**.

Two conclusions:
1. **The limiter is the corpus, not the model.** A single salient fact survives even a
   weak summarizer; the note can only earn its keep when the baseline actually *loses*
   facts. That requires a **multi-needle corpus** (many interleaved subtle facts, where
   even a good model can't keep all of them) — not just a weaker model.
2. **A weak model is a bad judge.** gpt-5.4-mini scored every arm 5/5, erasing the
   steering signal (the +0.91 gap gpt-5.5's judge found). The judge must stay strong
   (gpt-5.5 or a cross-family model); only the *summarizer* should be varied.

Next experiment: multi-needle histories (≥6 facts/case, count-based retention), strong
judge, to measure where arm A actually drops facts and the note rescues them.
