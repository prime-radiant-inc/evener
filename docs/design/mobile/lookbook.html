01 / Work in your hand

Compact does not have to mean limited.

Orientation before decoration

Study the relationship between navigation, item titles and supporting information. For Evener, the session title and useful context should lead; status and hub identity should support them.

Borrow the focus on compact work. Do not copy the issue model or assume the static image proves usability.

Composition is a primary workflow

Give the text and the next action a clear relationship. Secondary options should stay available without turning the keyboard-open screen into a control panel.

Linear’s July 2026 coding-session release makes change review and steering directly relevant precedents for Evener’s scope.

02 / Content with room to breathe

Structure without a box for everything.

A continuous reading surface

Study rich text as the main surface, with hierarchy inside the content. Evener needs this quality for long explanations and code, plus explicit boundaries for consequential decisions.

Transfer reading clarity. A note editor does not solve streaming, tool activity or approval state for us.

Reveal detail where it belongs

Things’ documented expanding tasks are a useful disclosure precedent: begin with a readable overview, then expose editing detail in context.

Study disclosure and hierarchy. This image is not evidence of the latest OS appearance; consult the published design updates for that history.

03 / Platform intelligence

One product. Two familiar ways to move.

Content stays grounded.

Apple’s materials guidance separates navigation and controls from content. Glass is not a treatment for every message.

Emphasis is selective.

Material Expressive research informs how shape, type and color direct attention. Evener should emphasize the action that matters, not every row.

Small ink. Ample touch.

Android accessibility guidance supports 48 dp touch regions. Visually quieter rows can still be easy to hit.

04 / The baseline to improve

Functional is not the visual finish line.

This prototype is included only to illustrate visual problems. Its controls and workflows are not evidence of intended Evener features.

  1. Too much container weight. Ordinary turns occupy distinct boxes rather than a continuous reading surface.
  2. Competing emphasis. Blue secondary controls draw attention away from the response.
  3. Unresolved rhythm. Activity, attribution and messages do not yet form a clear hierarchy.
  4. Expensive chrome. Status controls and the composer consume space without a coherent relationship to the content.

The next step is paired screen studies with the same realistic content on both platforms—not another isolated padding adjustment.
